Real-World Problem

The Real-World Problem: Why We Built This

The Problem
Diagnosis Takes Too Long
  • Autism affects 1 in 36 children worldwide, requiring urgent early detection.
  • Current tests require specialized hospital visits and lengthy behavioral exams.
  • Families often wait 2 to 3 years for an appointment, missing the most critical early development years.
The Idea
Eyes Reveal Attention
  • When looking at people, neurotypical children naturally look at eyes, faces, and smiles.
  • Children with Autism often avoid direct eye contact and look at background shapes or objects instead.
  • Eye-tracking creates a "map" of their gaze—giving an objective, non-invasive digital clue.
Our Solution
An Instant AI Assistant
  • We created an AI system that reads that gaze map and makes an instant, 98% accurate prediction.
  • Doctors do not need to write code: they simply upload the image to our web interface.
  • The tool explains exactly WHY it made the decision in simple, everyday language.
How It Works

How Our AI Works (4 Simple Steps)

Step 1
Clean the Image
  • Resize image to standard 224x224 pixels.
  • Light blur to smooth jagged computer lines.
  • Balance brightness so all images look uniform.
Step 2
Find Key Patterns
  • We use MobileNet, a proven vision AI.
  • It examines lines, curves, and gaze dots.
  • Converts the image into 1,024 descriptive numbers.
Step 3
Keep What Matters
  • 1,024 numbers is too many and can cause confusion.
  • PCA algorithm selects the 250 best signals.
  • Retains over 95% of the information while making it 4x faster.
Step 4
Team of Models
  • Two smart models (SVM + KNN) cast their votes.
  • A "manager" model (Logistic Regression) makes the final call.
  • Outputs the exact percentage chance of ASD.

Why It Works
Key Architectural Strengths
  • Two Models are Better Than One: By combining SVM and KNN, the two algorithms check each other's work so one model's mistake gets caught.
  • No Memorizing (Overfitting): Using PCA to reduce features prevented the model from simply memorizing images—it learns true gaze rules.
  • Super Fast: Predictions take less than half a second (400ms), making it ready for real clinics.

Explainable AI (Simple Words)

Explainability: Why Did the AI Make This Decision?

CHECK 1
Where Did They Look?
Peripheral vs Center

Children with Autism often fixate on the outer borders or empty background of an image rather than looking across the whole scene. Our system measures if their gaze is clustered away from central objects.

CHECK 2
Eye Contact & Faces
Avoiding Faces

When shown images of people, neurotypical children spend ~68% of their time looking at eyes and mouths. In ASD scanpaths, this drops significantly (often below 30%), showing clear face avoidance.

CHECK 3
Smooth vs Jerky Jumps
Restless Movements

Instead of following calm, smooth lines between objects, children with Autism often make short, scattered, zigzag eye movements. The system flags this high path jitter as a key indicator.

Handbook & User Guide

System Handbook: Instructions for Users & Clinic Staff

File Formats
What Images Can Be Uploaded?
  • Supports standard PNG, JPG, and JPEG files.
  • Compatible with raw gaze plots, line scanpaths, and colored heatmaps.
  • Any resolution works—the server automatically standardizes sizing to 224x224.
Reliability
Built-In Safety & Fallback
  • If trained model files are not installed, the app NEVER crashes.
  • It automatically runs in "simulation mode" to allow offline UI demonstrations.
  • A clear banner informs the doctor whether live weights or simulation mode is active.
Responsibility
Screening Aid vs Final Diagnosis
  • The portal explicitly states it is a screening assistant, not a replacement for a doctor.
  • Provides a confidence score so clinicians can see if a result is borderline or strong.
  • Recommends formal ADOS-2 testing when a high-risk pattern is detected.
